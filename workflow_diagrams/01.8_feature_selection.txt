================================================================================
  01.8 — Feature Selection: rdmoldes 50-descriptor set — Workflow Diagram
  src/feature_selection/feature_selection.py
================================================================================

GOAL
────
  Select a global (endpoint-agnostic) subset of the paper's 50 rdmoldes
  descriptors (316 expanded features) for the RF/LightGBM/FCNN data-quantity
  + noise study.

  Scope: rdkit featureset only. FCFP4/ECFP4 fingerprints are untouched.

  Granularity: every keep/drop decision is made per DESCRIPTOR (one of the 50
  named things — 44 single-feature scalars + 6 multi-feature vector
  descriptors, e.g. AUTOCORR2D -> 192 features), never per individual
  expanded feature. See STEP 0b for why.


INPUT
─────
  data/processed/section3_feat.pkl  (from 01.5)
  feat[ep]['rdkit']  — (N, 316) rdmoldes features, per endpoint
  ENDPOINTS_MODEL = HLM, MDR1, SOL, RLM  (PPB_H/PPB_R excluded — too sparse)

  Deduplication (steps 1-5 only): many compounds are tested across more than
  one of the 4 endpoints. Naively row-stacking all 4 endpoints' matrices to
  study descriptor redundancy would count those molecules 2-4x, giving them
  outsized influence on what "typical" redundancy looks like. So steps 1-5
  (variance, MI, correlation, VIF) pool the 3509 UNIQUE molecules only,
  deduplicated by canonical SMILES (X_all). Step 6 (RFE) and the R^2 audits
  switch back to each endpoint's own full, non-deduplicated rows against its
  own target — that's real modelling data, not a redundancy-estimation
  concern, so there's no reason to throw duplicate rows away there.
  Verified this mattered: with dedup, correlation-prune's R^2 cost dropped
  from -0.023 to -0.014 vs baseline — duplicate-weighted estimates were
  making descriptors look more redundant than they actually are.


STEP 0 — Descriptor map + constant filter (Section 1)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  rdmoldes_descriptor_map(): maps the 50 named descriptors to their column
  ranges in the 316-feature rdmoldes() output (in the exact order rdmoldes()
  concatenates them — 44 scalars, then PEOE_VSA(14)/SMR_VSA(10)/SlogP_VSA(12)
  /MQNs(42)/CrippenDescriptors(2)/AUTOCORR2D(192)).

  drop_constant_descriptors(X_all, descriptor_map, threshold=1e-8):
    For each descriptor, np.var() across all 3509 molecules on every one of
    its features. Dropped only if EVERY feature has variance <= threshold —
    a descriptor with even one informative feature is kept whole (never
    partially dropped).

  Result: 50 -> 48 descriptors, 316 -> 314 features.
  Dropped: CalcPBF, CalcSpherocityIndex — both are exactly 0.0 for every
  molecule, a direct consequence of the flat z=0 conformers inherited from
  the source SDFs (ADR-011), not an unrelated RDKit quirk:
    PBF = mean deviation of atoms from their own best-fit plane, already
          exactly the xy-plane when z=0
    Spherocity Index = 3*lambda_min / (lambda1+lambda2+lambda3) over the
          eigenvalues of the atomic coordinate covariance matrix — lambda_min
          is exactly 0 when the z-column has zero variance
  (CalcAsphericity/CalcEccentricity survive this filter — they use the
  mass-weighted inertia tensor instead, whose two in-plane principal moments
  stay generically distinct even for a flat molecule.)


STEP 0b — Diagnostic: descriptors vs. raw features (Section 1b)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  QUESTION: steps 1-5 below make every decision at the DESCRIPTOR level — a
  192-feature block like AUTOCORR2D is kept or dropped whole, never split
  across its own columns. Would selecting directly among the 316 raw feature
  columns instead be better?

  What raw-feature selection would look like: no PCA reduction (a raw scalar
  column already IS its own single value), no CCA (with k=1 on both sides,
  canonical correlation between two raw scalar columns is just |Pearson r|),
  VIF operating on real uncompressed columns instead of a single PC1 proxy.
  Strictly more expressive — 316 independent decisions instead of 50.

  WHY NOT DONE — checked directly, not just argued:
  mutual_info_regression() computed per raw column (no PCA, no descriptor
  grouping) against all 4 endpoints, max MI across endpoints per column, top
  30 raw features by that score inspected directly.

  Result: a large fraction of the top 30 raw features come from AUTOCORR2D
  alone — individually UNNAMED bins (e.g. "AUTOCORR2D position 47"), and not
  clustered in a small interpretable sub-range (spread across the full
  0-192 span, i.e. not "one informative sub-region of AUTOCORR2D" that a
  chemist could point to and explain).

  CONCLUSION: raw-feature-level selection would trade away interpretability
  for a small amount of extra dimensionality precision — the output would be
  dominated by scattered, anonymous AUTOCORR2D autocorrelation-lag bins
  rather than named, chemist-readable descriptors like TPSA or PEOE_VSA.
  Descriptor-level selection was kept as the primary methodology on that
  basis: every step below (PCA/CCA/VIF/RFE) treats a named descriptor as the
  atomic unit specifically to guarantee the final output is a short list of
  physically-meaningful quantities, not a bag of fingerprint-like bin
  indices with no story behind them. This diagnostic doesn't re-run — it's a
  one-time justification for the whole notebook's design, not a step in the
  pipeline itself.


STEP 1 — Descriptor components (Section 2)
──────────────────────────────────────────
  MECHANISM — descriptor_component_matrix(X_all, kept_map, n_components=5):
    For each of the 48 surviving descriptors:
      1. StandardScaler().fit_transform() on just that descriptor's own
         feature columns (z-score every feature to mean 0, std 1 — puts a
         44-scalar family and a 0-1-bounded VSA family on comparable scales
         before PCA, which is scale-sensitive)
      2. PCA(n_components=min(5, n_features)).fit_transform()
         -> scalar descriptors (44 of them) always reduce to k=1 (their own
            z-scored value — PCA on a single column is a no-op beyond
            standardizing)
         -> vector descriptors reduce to up to 5 components: PEOE_VSA(14->5),
            SMR_VSA(10->5), SlogP_VSA(12->5), MQNs(42->5), Crippen(2->2),
            AUTOCORR2D(192->5)

  WHY PCA HERE, AND WHY TOP-K NOT JUST PC1:
    Steps 2 (MI) and 3 (correlation-prune) need to compare/score descriptors
    of very different native widths (1 feature vs. 192 features) on equal
    footing. Collapsing straight to a single PC1 per descriptor would judge
    a 192-feature block's relevance/redundancy on one coincidentally
    informative-or-not axis — real signal or real redundancy living in a
    later component (PC2-PC5) would be invisible. Keeping the top 5
    components per descriptor means correlation-prune and MI both "see" more
    of what a wide descriptor actually contains, not just its dominant mode
    of variation.
    VIF is the one deliberate exception (Step 3) — see that step for why.

  This step produces no drops by itself — it's the shared representation
  that Steps 2 and 3 (MI, correlation-prune) both consume.


STEP 2 — Mutual information (Section 2, same section as Step 1)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  MECHANISM — mutual_info_per_descriptor(component_map, y_by_endpoint):
    For each descriptor, for each of the 4 endpoints:
      mutual_info_regression(descriptor's component matrix, that endpoint's
      target) -> one MI value per component -> take the MAX across that
      descriptor's own components (so a descriptor isn't underrated just
      because PC1 is uninformative while PC3 is not)
    Then take the MAX across the 4 endpoints per descriptor — "a descriptor
    is useful if it's useful for ANY endpoint," matching the project's
    per-endpoint modelling decision (each endpoint trains its own model, so
    a descriptor that only helps SOL shouldn't be penalized for not helping
    HLM too).

  Output: mi_max (one score per descriptor) — this is the score used as the
  tiebreak in EVERY downstream elimination step (correlation-prune, VIF,
  and to a lesser extent inspected alongside RFE). It is itself never used
  to drop a descriptor directly — MI only ever breaks ties between
  descriptors that some OTHER criterion (correlation, VIF, low gain) has
  already flagged as expendable relative to another specific descriptor.


STEP 3 — Correlation prune (Section 3)
────────────────────────────────────────
  MECHANISM — correlation_prune(component_map, mi_max, threshold=0.9):
    For every pair of the 48 descriptors, compute the first canonical
    correlation between their top-component sets:
      _canonical_correlation(A, B):
        k = min(A.n_components, B.n_components)
        A_c, B_c = sklearn.cross_decomposition.CCA(n_components=k)
                     .fit_transform(A, B)
        return |corrcoef(A_c[:,0], B_c[:,0])|
    Pairs with canonical correlation > 0.9 are sorted most-correlated first.
    For each pair still both "alive," drop whichever has the LOWER mi_max,
    keep the other. (Most-correlated-first ordering means the strongest
    redundancies are resolved before weaker, possibly now-moot ones.)

  WHY CCA, NOT PLAIN PEARSON ON PC1s:
    PCA (Step 1) answers a WITHIN-descriptor question: "what are this
    descriptor's own dominant axes of variation?" CCA answers a BETWEEN-
    descriptor question: "how correlated are these two descriptors with
    each other?" — using each one's full top-5 component set jointly,
    rather than only comparing their first components to each other. It's
    the standard generalization of Pearson correlation from two single
    variables to two multi-dimensional variable sets: CCA finds the linear
    combination of A's components and the linear combination of B's
    components that correlate most strongly with each other, and that
    correlation is what gets compared to the 0.9 threshold. Two descriptors
    could have uncorrelated PC1s yet be almost entirely redundant once you
    let PC2-PC5 combine — CCA catches that, plain PC1-to-PC1 Pearson
    wouldn't.
    (When both descriptors are scalars, k=1 on both sides and this
    collapses to plain |Pearson r| — no information lost for the 44
    single-feature descriptors, the generalization only matters for the 6
    wide ones.)

  KNOWN LIMITATION (explicitly documented, not silently accepted):
    CCA only finds the strongest LINEAR combination correlation. Two
    descriptors with genuine nonlinear (but real) dependence could pass this
    check as "not redundant" when they actually are. Not fixed with a
    nonlinear measure (e.g. distance correlation) because of the added
    O(n^2) compute over ~3500 molecules AND because VIF (Step 4) and the
    RFE/R^2 audit (Step 5) downstream both re-check whatever this step
    decides against real model performance — a wrong call here isn't
    silently trusted, it gets a second and third chance to be caught.

  Result: 48 -> 27 descriptors, 314 -> 92 features.
  Mean CV R^2: 0.4057 (was 0.4193 before any pruning) = 97% of ceiling.


STEP 4 — VIF prune (Section 4)
──────────────────────────────
  MECHANISM — descriptor_pc1_matrix(component_map):
    Unlike Steps 2-3, VIF collapses each descriptor down to ONLY its first
    principal component (pc1_df: one column per descriptor).

  WHY PC1-ONLY HERE, UNLIKE MI/CORRELATION:
    VIF is inherently a single-variable-vs-the-rest statistic: VIF(X_i) =
    1 / (1 - R^2) where R^2 comes from regressing X_i on every other
    variable in the set. That definition doesn't generalize to "a
    descriptor's whole 5-component block" without changing what VIF means —
    there's no natural multivariate-vs-multivariate VIF. So this is the one
    deliberate place PC1-only is kept rather than extended to top-k, and
    it's a design choice, not an oversight.

  MECHANISM — vif_prune(pc1_df, mi_max, threshold=5.0, tie_margin=0.05):
    Iteratively:
      1. variance_inflation_factor() (statsmodels) computed for every
         currently-remaining descriptor's PC1, against all OTHER remaining
         descriptors' PC1s at once (not just pairwise) — multicollinearity
         against the whole surviving set, re-derived fresh each iteration
         since VIF changes as descriptors leave.
      2. If max VIF <= 5.0, stop.
      3. Otherwise: descriptors within 5% (tie_margin) of the max VIF are
         treated as tied; among those, drop the one with the LOWEST mi_max.
      4. Repeat.
    vif_mi_table() is also produced as a side-by-side VIF+MI snapshot of the
    whole 27-descriptor candidate pool BEFORE any elimination — lets a human
    sanity-check that the automated iterative process isn't about to cut
    something that looks important on inspection.

  NOTE — VIF can legitimately be `inf`: RDKit's ring-count descriptors
  satisfy EXACT accounting identities (e.g. CalcNumAromaticRings =
  CalcNumAromaticCarbocycles + CalcNumAromaticHeterocycles, verified
  np.allclose=True on real data). When all three terms of such an identity
  are still in the pool, the third is perfectly reconstructable (R^2=1
  exactly) -> VIF=inf. This is VIF correctly identifying the most redundant
  possible descriptor, not a numerical bug.

  Result: 27 -> 16 descriptors, 92 -> 40 features.
  Mean CV R^2: 0.3824 (was 0.4057 after correlation-prune) = 91% of ceiling.
  16 survivors: CalcTPSA, CalcNumLipinskiHBD, CalcNumHeteroatoms,
  CalcNumAmideBonds, CalcNumAliphaticHeterocycles, CalcNumAromaticCarbocycles,
  CalcNumAromaticRings, CalcNumSaturatedRings, CalcNumRotatableBonds,
  CalcChi3v, PEOE_VSA, SlogP_VSA, CalcEccentricity, CalcInertialShapeFactor,
  CalcPMI1, CalcPMI3 (the last 4 are ADR-011 flat-2D-geometry descriptors —
  none of them make it past Step 5).


STEP 5 — LightGBM recursive descriptor elimination (Section 5)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  MECHANISM — run_descriptor_rfe(X_by_endpoint, y_by_endpoint,
                                  rfe_descriptor_map, min_descriptors=1,
                                  cv=5):
    Operates on REAL features now, not PCA components — each endpoint's own
    full (non-deduplicated) rows, using the 40 real rdmoldes columns of the
    16 surviving descriptors.
    Loop, while len(remaining) > min_descriptors:
      1. Fit ONE LGBMRegressor per endpoint (n_estimators=200,
         importance_type='gain') on that endpoint's own X/y for the
         currently-remaining features.
      2. 5-fold CV R^2 (KFold, shuffle, random_state=42) logged for THIS
         step's descriptor set, per endpoint and averaged -> this is the
         trace point plotted in Step 6, BEFORE any removal this round.
      3. Importance per descriptor: take each descriptor's features' max
         gain-importance from that endpoint's fitted model, then MAX across
         the 4 endpoints (again: "useful for ANY endpoint" survives) ->
         one importance score per remaining descriptor.
      4. Drop the descriptor with the LOWEST importance score. Repeat.
    Runs all the way to 1 descriptor (MIN_DESCRIPTORS=1) rather than
    stopping at an arbitrary floor — earlier runs used MIN_BLOCKS=10 or
    min_descriptors=3 with no data-driven justification, and would have
    missed the second, much larger cliff later in the trace (see Step 6).

  GAIN-IMPORTANCE, PRECISELY:
    "Gain" = the total reduction in the model's loss function attributable
    to a feature, summed over every split in every tree that uses it — a
    measure of how much a feature actually improved predictions, not how
    often it happened to be used. LightGBM's LGBMRegressor computes this
    internally (booster.feature_importance(importance_type='gain')) whenever
    importance_type='gain' is passed at construction; feature_importances_
    then returns that array. Contrast with 'split' (scikit-learn/LightGBM's
    DEFAULT if importance_type isn't set): a raw count of how many times a
    feature was used as a split point anywhere in the ensemble, regardless
    of how much any individual split actually helped.

  *** BUG FOUND AND FIXED MID-PROJECT ***
    run_descriptor_rfe originally constructed LGBMRegressor WITHOUT
    importance_type='gain' — every surrounding comment/docstring described
    gain-importance, but the code was silently using scikit-learn's
    default, which for LGBMRegressor is 'split'-count.
    CAUGHT BY: Section 8's shadowing diagnostic (see Step 5b below) —
    CalcEccentricity had EXACTLY ZERO importance in the full 316-feature
    model across all 4 endpoints, despite being nearly rank-identical
    (Spearman rho=1.0) to CalcNPR2, which had real nonzero importance.
    Exactly the signature of split-count (a feature never chosen as a split
    point gets exactly 0, gain or no gain) being mistaken for a
    gain-importance finding.
    FIXED: importance_type='gain' set explicitly in run_descriptor_rfe's
    LGBMRegressor construction (feature_selection.py line ~306), whole
    pipeline re-run top to bottom. This CHANGED the final 5-descriptor
    selection: CalcEccentricity/CalcPMI3 (ADR-011 flat-geometry descriptors)
    dropped out, replaced by CalcNumAromaticCarbocycles/CalcChi3v.

  CV_FOLDS = 5, not 3: an earlier run with 3-fold gave visibly noisy,
  non-monotonic step-to-step swings (values zigzagging as descriptors were
  removed) inconsistent with a real information-loss trend — 5-fold trades
  some speed for a cleaner trace, which matters because Step 6 reads this
  trace by eye.

  Result: 16 -> 1 descriptor, full trace logged (16 steps).


STEP 5b — Diagnostic: gain-importance shadowing/masking (Section 8)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  QUESTION: RFE trusts gain-importance to decide what's safe to drop. If two
  descriptors are redundant in a way TREES can't see (a tree only ever asks
  "which side of a threshold"), RFE could keep the wrong one purely by
  chance of elimination order — without it ever showing up as an R^2 drop,
  since the model still has an informationally-equivalent descriptor
  available.

  THREE CHECKS, run against the whole Step-4 candidate pool (not hardcoded
  to one pair):
    1. Rank-correlation redundancy CCA/VIF may have missed: Spearman rho
       between every pair of survivors/dropped-descriptors' PC1s — CCA/VIF
       are both fundamentally LINEAR checks; two descriptors can be only
       moderately linearly correlated (canonical r < 0.9, passing both
       checks) while being EXACTLY rank-identical (rho=1.0) — invisible to
       any tree model, even though a smooth model like FCNN could still
       tell them apart. (This is exactly how the gain-vs-split bug above
       was caught: CalcEccentricity vs CalcNPR2, rho=1.0.)
    2. RF vs. LightGBM importance divergence: do the two tree ensembles even
       agree on what matters, in the full 316-feature model? Checked as a
       sanity bound on how much to trust LightGBM-only RFE as "what these
       models find important" in general (FCNN excluded — no native
       importance metric without a separate permutation-importance pass).
    3. Direct swap test: for any Step-5-selected descriptor with a
       near-rank-identical relative among the dropped descriptors, swap one
       for the other and re-measure CV R^2. Near-zero delta = the
       descriptor's presence in the final set is substitutable, not
       uniquely load-bearing.

  RESULT after the gain-importance fix: the severe case (CalcEccentricity /
  CalcNPR2) is gone — neither is in the corrected final 5. One milder
  near-rank-identical pair remains among the final descriptors (swap delta
  -0.004, minor) — expected residual risk, not alarming: CCA/VIF are still
  linear checks, so some tree-invisible-but-real redundancy slipping past
  them on every run is the expected outcome this diagnostic exists to catch,
  not evidence of a new bug.


STEP 6 — Inspect the trace, pick a cutoff (Section 6) -> Figure
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  PLOT (section_fs_rfe_trace.png): combines the constant-filter baseline
  (48 desc), the correlation-prune stage (27 desc), and the FULL RFE trace
  (16 -> 1 desc) into one continuous curve: mean CV R^2 (y) vs. number of
  descriptors remaining (x, inverted so it reads left-to-right as pruning
  progresses).

  HOW THE CUTOFF WAS PICKED — by eye, on the trace, using two features of
  the curve:
    (a) From 16 down to 5 descriptors: R^2 declines gently and roughly
        monotonically, each step costing <= 0.008 R^2 — normal, expected
        information loss as weak descriptors leave.
    (b) At 5 -> 4 descriptors: a -0.061 drop (dropping SlogP_VSA) — roughly
        8x any single step in the 16->5 range. This is the first REAL elbow.
    SELECTED_N_DESCRIPTORS = 5 — the smallest descriptor count BEFORE this
    first cliff.
    (Continuing the trace further reveals a SECOND, much larger cliff at
    3 -> 2 descriptors: -0.221 (dropping PEOE_VSA) — the single biggest drop
    anywhere in the whole trace. Running to min_descriptors=1 rather than
    stopping early was necessary specifically to see this second cliff, not
    just the first — see Step 7 for why RFE's picks in this N<=5 tail
    region are not actually trustworthy despite this elbow-picking logic
    looking reasonable in isolation.)

  Final 5-descriptor selection (selected_descriptors.json): CalcTPSA,
  CalcNumAromaticCarbocycles, CalcChi3v, PEOE_VSA, SlogP_VSA.
  Mean CV R^2: 0.3555 (was 0.3824 after VIF) = 85% of ceiling.
  16 -> 5 descriptors, 40 -> 29 features.


STEP 7 — Standalone contribution: was RFE's tail-end order reliable?
       (Section 8b, feeds the bar-plot numbers directly)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  DIFFERENT QUESTION FROM STEP 5b: Step 5b asked whether a SURVIVOR is
  redundant with something already dropped. This step asks: once RFE got
  down to its last few descriptors, did gain-importance keep dropping the
  LEAST valuable one — or just the one LightGBM happened to lean on least
  AT THAT PARTICULAR STEP, given whatever else was already in the model?

  MECHANISM — evaluate_descriptor_set() called once per descriptor ALONE
  (a single-descriptor model, no RFE, no other descriptors present):
    For each of the 5 final descriptors: 5-fold CV R^2 of a LightGBM
    trained on JUST that descriptor's own real features, per endpoint,
    averaged.
    standalone_r2 results (mean CV R^2, solo):
      PEOE_VSA                     0.265   <- strongest alone
      SlogP_VSA                    0.255   <- 2nd strongest alone
      CalcNumAromaticCarbocycles   ~0.06-ish (weak)
      CalcTPSA                     0.038
      CalcChi3v                    0.040   <- weakest alone

  THIS IS HOW "TOP-N BY SOLO R^2" IS DEFINED for the bar-plot's last two
  bars: ranked_by_standalone = descriptors sorted by this solo score,
  descending. Top-2 by solo R^2 = {PEOE_VSA, SlogP_VSA}. Top-3 adds
  whichever standalone-ranked descriptor is 3rd (CalcNumAromaticCarbocycles).
  These sets are NOT taken from any RFE trace step — they're built directly
  from the standalone ranking, then evaluate_descriptor_set() is re-run on
  that exact N-descriptor set to get its own real (not summed-from-solo)
  joint CV R^2 — descriptors together are not additive, so this joint
  re-evaluation is what actually produced the 0.3545 (N=3) and 0.3458 (N=2)
  numbers in the bar plot, not an arithmetic combination of the solo scores.

  DIRECT COMPARISON — RFE's own trace step at N=4/3/2 vs. the top-N-by-
  standalone set of the same size, both evaluated the same way:
                  RFE's own picks          Top-N by standalone R^2
    N=4    (drops SlogP_VSA first)    vs   keeps the two VSA descriptors
    N=3    (still carries              vs   {PEOE_VSA, SlogP_VSA,
            CalcEccentricity/PMI3           CalcNumAromaticCarbocycles}
            under the pre-fix run)          R^2=0.3545 (85%)
    N=2    {CalcTPSA, CalcChi3v}       vs   {PEOE_VSA, SlogP_VSA}
           R^2=0.066 (barely above         R^2=0.3458 (82%)
           noise)
  Standalone-ranked beats RFE's own trace step by 0.043-0.279 R^2 at every
  N checked, worst at N=2.

  WHY THIS HAPPENS (not a metric-choice bug, a structural property of
  greedy marginal elimination): gain-importance is a MARGINAL measure — how
  much a descriptor helps GIVEN whatever else is already in the model.
  PEOE_VSA and SlogP_VSA both encode overlapping information (surface area
  partitioned by a physicochemical property — charge for one, logP
  contribution for the other). Once one is in the model, gain-importance
  undersells how much the OTHER would matter on its own if the first were
  removed — so RFE's greedy path drops SlogP_VSA at 5->4 and PEOE_VSA at
  3->2 (the two individually-strongest descriptors in the whole pool),
  BEFORE dropping CalcTPSA/CalcChi3v (the two individually-weakest), which
  are what's left standing at N=1. Once you're down to a handful of
  descriptors that are individually strong but partially correlated with
  each other, marginal importance is the wrong lens for "what's safe to
  cut" — RFE's broad pruning (48->16->5) stays trustworthy throughout (every
  step there removes descriptors that are genuinely weak, not just
  currently shadowed); the failure mode is specific to this N<=5 tail where
  every survivor looks similarly strong to a marginal metric.


STEP 8 — Takeaway table + bar plot (Section 9, 9b) -> Figure
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  The bar plot (section_fs_variance_explained_by_stage.png) draws exactly
  the 6 rows below — this is where every one of its bars/numbers comes from:

  | Stage                     | Descriptors | Features | Mean CV R^2 | % ceiling |
  |----------------------------|-----|-----|--------|------|
  | No pruning (post-constant) | 48  | 314 | 0.4193 | 100% |  <- Step 0
  | Post correlation-prune CCA | 27  | 92  | 0.4057 | 97%  |  <- Step 3
  | Post VIF-prune             | 16  | 40  | 0.3824 | 91%  |  <- Step 4
  | RFE elbow, N=5             | 5   | 29  | 0.3556 | 85%  |  <- Step 6
  | Top-3 by solo R^2          | 3   | 27  | 0.3545 | 85%  |  <- Step 7
  | Top-2 by solo R^2 (RECO)   | 2   | 26  | 0.3458 | 82%  |  <- Step 7

  ceiling = the "No pruning" row's mean_cv_r2 (0.4193); every % is
  row.mean_cv_r2 / ceiling. n_features per row = sum of descriptor_map
  feature counts for whichever descriptors are in that row's set (NOT
  re-derived from any model, just the known layout from Step 0).
  This table -> DataFrame -> data/processed/section_fs_stage_summary.csv
  -> bar chart, one bar per row, annotated with (features, descriptors) and
  the %-of-ceiling printed inside each bar.

  RECOMMENDATION: N=2 (PEOE_VSA + SlogP_VSA). Not a hand-wavy compromise —
  the actual point of diminishing returns: standalone-ranked (doesn't
  inherit RFE's tail-end unreliability from Step 7), 96% of what the full
  16->5 RFE pipeline achieves (0.3458 vs 0.3556) using 26 of the 5-descriptor
  set's 29 features, and every descriptor added past it (by either ranking
  method) buys single-digit-R^2-point returns at best. RFE's own 5-descriptor
  selected_descriptors.json remains available as a defensible alternative if
  a downstream model can spare the extra 3 descriptors for +5 ceiling points.

  Physical meaning:
    PEOE_VSA  — van der Waals surface area binned by Gasteiger partial
                charge (14 bins): how much surface is electron-rich vs.
                electron-poor — polarity / H-bonding character.
    SlogP_VSA — same surface-area partitioning, binned by each atom's
                Crippen logP contribution (12 bins): how much surface is
                lipophilic vs. not, and where.
    Together: a compact "polar surface vs. lipophilic surface" fingerprint —
    directly mechanistic for all 4 modelled endpoints (HLM/RLM metabolic
    clearance, MDR1 efflux recognition, aqueous solubility are all
    textbook lipophilicity/polarity-governed ADME properties).
